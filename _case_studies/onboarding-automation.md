## Background

A 300-employee logistics company was hiring 15-20 new warehouse and operations staff per month. New hires spent their first week fielding basic questions — where forms were, what the dress code was, how to request time off — mostly through HR staff who were stretched thin across multiple sites.

HR estimated that roughly a third of a new hire's first week was spent waiting for answers rather than actually training.

## The Approach

The company built a simple AI chatbot trained on their existing HR documentation — the employee handbook, benefits FAQ, and onboarding checklist — and made it available to new hires from day one via a link in their welcome email.

Key decisions that mattered:
- **Scope was deliberately narrow.** The bot only answered from approved HR documents, and was instructed to say "I don't know, ask your manager" rather than guess.
- **It escalated ambiguous or sensitive questions** (pay disputes, harassment concerns) directly to a human HR contact rather than attempting to answer.
- **It was framed as a supplement, not a replacement**, for the in-person onboarding buddy system already in place.

## Results

Over a 4-month rollout period:
- New-hire time-to-productivity dropped from an average of 12 working days to about 7
- HR fielded roughly 40% fewer repetitive onboarding questions
- New-hire satisfaction scores (measured via a day-30 survey) rose slightly, which the HR team attributed to faster answers reducing early frustration

## Key Lessons

1. **Narrow scope beat broad ambition.** Early attempts to have the bot answer general workplace questions produced inconsistent results; restricting it to HR documentation made it far more reliable.
2. **Escalation paths were essential.** Employees trusted the tool more once they knew it would hand off sensitive topics rather than attempt an answer.
3. **The bot didn't replace people — it removed friction.** The onboarding buddy system stayed in place; the chatbot simply handled the repetitive, low-stakes questions that used to interrupt both new hires and HR staff.

The company's HR lead summarized the outcome simply: fewer interruptions for staff, faster answers for new hires, and no reduction in the human support that mattered most.
