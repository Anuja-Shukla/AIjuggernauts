## This issue: where to actually start

Faculty new to AI tools often try to do everything at once — lesson plans, grading, discussion questions, feedback — and end up overwhelmed within a week. The educators who stick with it tend to start narrower.

**Pick one recurring task.** Something you do every week that eats time but doesn't require much judgment — drafting a first pass at quiz questions, generating discussion prompts, or writing differentiated versions of a worksheet. Get comfortable prompting for that one thing before expanding.

**Build a reusable prompt.** Once you find a prompt that works well for your subject and grade level, save it. Reuse it as a template rather than re-explaining your context every time.

**Treat outputs as drafts, not finals.** AI-generated content saves time on the first draft, not the final edit. Build in a quick review step rather than skipping it to save time — that's where quality control actually happens.
