A recurring theme in feedback from faculty using AI-generated case studies: the ones that land best aren't the most detailed — they're the ones built around a genuine, ambiguous decision.

## The mistake most AI-generated cases make

Ask most AI tools for "a case study on market segmentation" and you'll get a tidy company profile with a segmentation section bolted onto the end. Students read it, nod, and move on — there's nothing to actually decide.

The fix is structural: a good case starts with a person who has to make a call, under real constraints, with at least two options that both seem reasonable. The company background exists only to support that decision, not the other way around.

## What this looks like in practice

Instead of "This company has these five customer segments," a stronger prompt asks the AI to put a specific decision-maker in front of a specific choice — expand into a new segment now, or wait a quarter for more data — and to make both options genuinely defensible.

## A quick test for your own prompts

Before using an AI-generated case in class, check: could two reasonable students land on different answers and both be able to defend their reasoning? If the "right" answer is obvious from paragraph two, the case needs another pass.
